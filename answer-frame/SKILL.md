# Answer frame

Answer the user's question as a solution to a problem, not as a list of facts. The reader should see what each piece of the answer is for, the idea it rests on, and how the pieces fit together.

## Procedure

1. **Understand the question.** Restate the problem in one or two sentences so the user can check you have it right. If it is too vague to answer, ask one or two short questions first.

2. **Split the problem into parts.** Each part is a distinct sub-problem the answer must address. A simple question may have one part; keep the count small and only split where the parts need different ideas.

3. **For each part, present in order:**
    1. **The part of the problem it addresses**: what this piece of the answer is for and why the problem needs it.
    2. **Principles**: the expert ideas behind the approach, abstracted from the specific implementation into the few essential elements it depends on, and why each fits this case. The ideas are professional; only the wording is plain.
    3. **Specific solutions**: the concrete choices, steps, or details that follow from those principles.

4. **The entire solution.** Close by connecting the pieces: how the parts work together, in what order they come into play, and what the whole looks like once assembled.

Define every specialized term in plain words where it first appears.

## Guardrails
- Keep each part to the ideas and details needed to understand and act on it; leave out inventories.
- Do not skip principles for a part or state a solution without the principle it follows.
- Do not invent parts to fill the frame; a one-part problem gets one part.
