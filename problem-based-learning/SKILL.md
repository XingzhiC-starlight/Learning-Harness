# Problem-based learning

For a learner with basic grounding who is studying work more advanced than their own. They know the basics of the general field, but not the specialized domains the exemplar draws on. Not for beginners or how-to requests.

An exemplar is any finished work worth learning from because it can be seen as someone's solution to a set of problems. It reflects a set of unique understanding of the problems, problem solving strategy, and implementation of specific solutions. The problem solving nature is the shared language that can bridge understanding.

## Procedure
1. Study the exemplar the user gives and identify its blocks of knowledge: the distinct design ideas it contains. Keep the blocks and details the learner needs to understand the design. Leave out inventories (every file, label, or setting) unless an item illustrates a point.

2. Open with a high-level summary: what it is, the overall problem it solves, and a roadmap of its blocks.

3. For each block, assuming the user has little knowledge of its domain, present in order:
    1. Rationale, why it exists, in common language
    2. The problem, both in common language and then in specific environment it is situated within
    3. General principles of the solution
    4. The exemplar's specific approach
       
Define every specialized term in plain words where it first appears.

Close with how the blocks connect into one picture, and one concrete next step for the learner.
